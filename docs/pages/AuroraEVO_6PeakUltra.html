6 Peak Ultra Bead Tracking — Aurora EVO (5L)

Spectral QC: instrument-performance tracking from URCP-38-2K beads

Published

June 29, 2026

Secondary QC for the Cytek Aurora EVO from SPHERO URCP-38-2K Ultra Rainbow beads. The set resolves into 6 populations — blank particles (peak 1) plus 5 fluorescent rainbow levels (peaks 2–6). Note on the Aurora: daily QC adjusts each APD’s gain to pin bead MFI to a target, so raw MFI is held ~constant by design — the informative signals are the ones gain can’t compensate: sensitivity (Q & B), resolution, %CV, linearity, and spectral-signature stability. Heavy FCS processing is done once by scripts/Process6PeakUltra.R; this page reads the peak_metrics.csv archive (+ the daily-QC gain) so it stays light.

Only 4 run(s) so far — trends and the mean ± 2 SD control limits are preliminary and will firm up as more runs accumulate.

Detail

Sensitivity (Q & B)

Q = detection efficiency (photoelectrons per unit signal, gain-normalised) — a drop means the detector collects less light per event. B = background/noise term; intrinsic CV = the high-signal noise floor (bead + optics). Estimated per detector from CV² vs 1/MFI across the resolved peaks. Because Q = 1/slope blows up when the slope nears zero, fits that are ill-conditioned — near-flat or negative slope, R² < 0.8, a non-monotonic CV profile, or a brightest-peak CV > 15% — are suppressed (left blank) rather than reported as a phantom Q spike; the underlying cause then shows in the %CV panel.

NoteMethod & provenance — read me

This is the Q–B detector-sensitivity model of Hoffman & Wood (Wood JC, Cytometry 1998;33:260–266; Wood & Hoffman, Cytometry 1998;33:256–259; Chase & Hoffman, Cytometry 1998;33:267–279; Hoffman & Wood, Current Protocols in Cytometry Unit 1.20, 2007), the same model behind Spherotech’s STN-17 and BD’s CS&T Qr/Br. Total spread decomposes as CV² = CV_intr² + (1/Q)(1/S) + (B/Q)(1/S²); fitting CV² vs 1/S gives intrinsic CV (intercept), 1/Q (linear term) and B/Q (curvature).

The panel-wide values use gain-normalised MFI (S = MFI/gain), not MEF, so Q and B are a relative, non-normalised index — valid for single-detector trend monitoring (Levey-Jennings), not cross-detector comparison, and expect small steps when a bead lot changes. Absolute, gain-independent sensitivity lives in the MEF-unit Q/B and detection-limit (MEF) panels below, on the anchor detectors.

Q & B in MEF units (anchor detectors)

The Q/B heatmaps above are in gain-normalised MFI units — comparable across the full 64-detector panel, but arbitrary. On the anchor detectors (where the URCP-38-2K certificate gives a per-peak MEF) the same CV² regression is run against true MEF, yielding Q and B in absolute, gain-independent units matching the Spherotech STN-17 definition. The intrinsic CV (high-signal floor) is scale-invariant, so it should agree between the two fits — a built-in check.

The regression above uses every resolved peak. As an independent check, the literal STN-17 closed form uses just three populations — the blank (peak 1), the dimmest fluorescent bead carrying an assigned MEF (peak 3) and the brightest (peak 6). The blank carries no assigned MEF, so it is extrapolated through the per-run calibration, then Q = 1/(CV²_blank × MEF_blank). The two Q estimates won’t match exactly (3-point algebra vs. multi-point fit) but should track each other; large divergence flags a nonlinear detector.

%CV & noise floor

Peak 6 (brightest) uses robust %CV — it has a well-defined mean (~1–2% when healthy). The blank (peak 1) uses its absolute robust spread (IQR/1.349), not %CV: the blank sits near zero, so %CV explodes when the mean dips toward zero even though the actual spread is unchanged. A rising blank spread is a genuine noise-floor increase.
